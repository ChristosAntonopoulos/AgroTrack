## Purpose

How we run sprint planning meetings so every sprint starts with a clear goal and a realistic plan.

## Structure (90 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Recap of last sprint outcome and any carryover |
| 10–20 min | Confirm or set the sprint goal |
| 20–60 min | Walk through Ready PBIs, pull them into the sprint |
| 60–75 min | Identify risks, dependencies and unknowns |
| 75–90 min | Confirm the plan, assign initial ownership |

## Inputs

- A refined backlog (PBIs that meet [Definition of Ready](./Definition%20of%20Ready.md)).
- The previous sprint's review and retro notes.
- Current customer research signals from [Validation Findings](../04%20Customer%20Research/Validation%20Findings.md).
- Capacity from the team (holidays, time off, external commitments).

## Outputs

- A written **Sprint Goal** (one sentence).
- A list of PBIs committed for the sprint.
- Clear ownership of major PBIs.
- A list of known risks.

## Sprint goal examples

- **Sprint 0:** "Set up the team, the boards, the wiki, the demo, and the first 5 customer interviews."
- **Sprint 1:** "Owners can sign up, create an organisation, add fields and start a crop cycle on a demo environment."
- **Sprint 2:** "Producers can be invited, view fields and log activity from a phone."

## Rules

- Don't over-commit. Better to ship less than promised more.
- Every sprint has a goal that can be stated in one sentence.
- Surface blockers early — don't wait until day 10.

## Open Questions

- How do we handle bug fixes — inside the sprint or outside?
